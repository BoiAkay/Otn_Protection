Optical Transport Network Protection Explained

Every second, petabits of critical telecommunications, financial services, and cloud data traverse optical fiber networks. When an optical fiber is severed or an optical transceiver degrades, OTN Protection Switching automatically restores the entire traffic payload in under 50 milliseconds.

Fundamentals: Why and How Optical Networks Protect Themselves

⚡

The < 50ms Carrier SLA

Telecommunications networks demand "Carrier-Grade 5-Nines" (99.999% availability). When a physical fiber snaps, the network must detect the failure, coordinate across hundreds of kilometers, and switch paths in less than 50 milliseconds so human phone calls and video streams do not drop.

🌉

The Bridge (Head-End)

The Bridge is located at the Source Node. It determines how user traffic enters the transmission medium:
Permanent Bridge (1+1): Replicates data continuously onto both Working and Protection fibers.
Switched Bridge (1:N): Normally sends data only on Working; switches to Protection upon defect.

🔀

The Selector (Tail-End)

The Selector is located at the Destination Node. It acts as an optical/electrical switch choosing which incoming line supplies the client payload. If the Working line suffers Loss of Signal (LOS) or Signal Degrade (SD), the selector flips to the Protection line.

Traffic & Entity Terminology (ITU-T G.808.1 & G.873.1)

1. Normal Traffic Signal (Working) The primary high-priority client payload (e.g. 100GbE / ODU4) that is protected by the architecture.
2. Protection Entity (Backup) The secondary optical channel / path reserved to restore normal traffic when the working entity fails.
3. Extra Traffic (ET) Low-priority, pre-emptible traffic carried over an idle protection entity in 1:1 or 1:N architectures. Instantly dropped if a protection switch occurs.

1+1 Unidirectional Protection

In 1+1 Unidirectional switching, the Head-End (Node A) uses a Permanent Bridge (Optical Splitter) to continuously duplicate client data onto both Working and Protection fibers simultaneously. The Tail-End (Node B) monitors the Working fiber locally; if a defect occurs, Node B immediately switches its selector without consulting Node A.

Current Signal Flow: Normal Operation

Traffic enters Node A Tx, is split permanently to both paths. Node B selector is connected to Working path. Protection path carries duplicate standby flow.

Switching Trigger: Local Tail-End Defect (LOS / SD)
Bandwidth Efficiency: 50% (Dedicated 1:1 Redundancy)
Switching Speed: Fastest (< 10ms - No Handshake)
Revertive vs Non-Revertive:
• Revertive: When the repaired fiber becomes stable, the Wait-to-Restore (WTR) timer (5–12 mins) elapses and traffic reverts to Working.
• Non-Revertive (DNR): Traffic remains on Protection to avoid a second disruption hit.
Module 3

Subnetwork Connection (SNC) Monitoring Modes

How OTN decides when to switch: Inherent (SNC/I), Sublayer (SNC/S), and Non-Intrusive (SNC/N) monitoring.

SNC/I Inherent Monitoring

Server-Layer Monitored

SNC/I relies entirely on the server layer trail (such as the OTUk frame or higher-order ODUk server) to detect faults.

  • Trigger: OTUk-LOS, OTUk-LOF, OTUk-BDI.
  • Does not require ODUk TCM overhead.
  • Simple, low complexity for link-by-link protection.
MFAS bits 6-8: 1 1 1 (Server Layer)
SNC/S Recommended

Sublayer Monitored (TCM)

SNC/S assigns a dedicated Tandem Connection Monitoring (TCM1–TCM6) level specifically to the protection subnetwork.

  • Trigger: ODUkT-SSF, ODUkT-SSD, ODUkT-TIM.
  • Isolates faults to the protected subnetwork boundary.
  • Ignores upstream faults outside the protected zone.
MFAS bits 6-8: 0 0 1 – 1 1 0 (TCM1–TCM6)
SNC/N Non-Intrusive Monitoring

End-to-End Sniffing

SNC/Ne "sniffs" the end-to-end ODUk Path (ODUkP) overhead at intermediate protection switches without terminating or modifying the trail.

  • Trigger: ODUk-AIS, ODUk-OCI, ODUk-LCK, dDEG.
  • Useful for client subnetwork protection.
  • Cannot differentiate between local vs remote faults.
MFAS bits 6-8: 0 0 0 (ODUk Path level)

Module 5

ODU Shared Ring Protection (OSPRing / G.873.2)

Comparing the two standardized ring restoration mechanisms: Wrapping vs Steering.

1. Wrapping Mechanism

Adjacent Loopback

When a link breaks between adjacent Node A and Node B, both nodes execute a local U-turn. Node A wraps Eastbound traffic onto the Westbound protection ring.

+ Advantages:
• Instant local action (< 50ms). Only adjacent nodes need to flip switches.
- Disadvantages:
• "Trombone Effect": Signals traverse the ring up to 3 times, tripling propagation delay.

2. Steering Mechanism

Long-Haul Optimized

When a failure occurs, ring nodes broadcast APS notifications to the original Source and Destination nodes, which redirect the traffic the opposite way around the ring.

+ Advantages:
• Minimal latency: Direct path around the ring without unnecessary looping. Essential for rings >1500km.
- Requirements:
• Requires full ring node coordination via APS protocol before rerouting.

🛡️ Misconnection Prevention: The Squelch (Squash) Table

In multi-node ring networks experiencing multiple simultaneous cable cuts or node isolating failures, wrapped traffic could accidentally be delivered to the wrong customer node. ITU-T G.873.2 mandates a Squelch Table in every network element. If a destination node is severed, intermediate nodes insert ODUk-OCI (Open Connection Indication) or ODUk-AIS to intentionally drop the traffic rather than risk private data leakage.

Standards Cross-Reference & Key Takeaways

Key Rule Summary

  • 1+1 Unidir: Permanent Bridge, Local Selector, No APS required. Fastest switch.
  • 1+1 Bidir: Permanent Bridge, APS protocol synchronizes both selectors for symmetric latency.
  • 1:1 Bidir: Switched Bridge, supports Extra Traffic on backup line, APS protocol required.
  • 1:N Bidir: Single protection shared by N working lines. Priority table resolves race conditions.
  • SNC/S: Uses Tandem Connection Monitoring (TCM) to protect sub-domains without end-to-end impact.
  • Switching SLA: Total switch time must remain under 50 ms.

#1

1+1 Unidirectional • SNC/I (Inherent Monitoring)

No APS Individual ODUkP/ODUkT Trigger: ODU SSF/SSD
Live Animation

Server Layer Monitoring (SNC/I): Protection switching is triggered by defects detected in the server layer (OTUk or HO ODUk). The protected entity is the ODUkP (path) or ODUkT (tandem).

✓ Key Characteristics:
• Bridge: Permanent optical splitter at Node A
• Switching: Unidirectional (local tail-end decision)
• Speed: Fastest (< 10ms) - no APS coordination
• Trigger: ODU SSF (Signal Fail) or SSD (Signal Degrade)
• MFAS bits 6-8: Not applicable (no APS channel)
#2

1+1 Bidirectional • SNC/I (Inherent Monitoring)

APS: 111 Individual Server: OTUk/HO ODUk
Bidirectional Coordination

Bidirectional Coordination: Unlike unidirectional, both Node A and Node B coordinate via APS protocol (MFAS bits 6-8 = 111) to ensure symmetric switching in both directions.

⚡ APS Coordination Benefits:
• Equal round-trip latency maintained
• Prevents asymmetric routing issues
• MFAS 111 = Server layer (OTUk) APS channel
• Switch time: < 50ms (includes APS handshake)
#3

1:n Bidirectional • SNC/I (Shared Protection)

APS: 111 1:N Switched Bridge Priority Arbitration
N Working Channels Share 1 Protection

Shared Protection Economy: Multiple working entities (N) share a single protection entity. When any working path fails, the switched bridge connects that specific working signal to protection.

⚠️ Priority Resolution:
If multiple working paths fail simultaneously, the APS priority table determines which signal gets protection first.
• Byte 2 = Signal ID (1-254)
• Lower ID wins during equal-priority conflicts
#4

1+1 Unidirectional • SNC/Ne (Non-intrusive End-to-end)

No APS ODUkP Monitoring Trigger: ODU TSF/TSD
End-to-end Path Monitoring

Non-intrusive End-to-end (SNC/Ne): "Sniffs" the ODUk Path (ODUkP) overhead at intermediate nodes without terminating the trail. Monitors end-to-end path quality.

✓ Key Characteristics:
• Monitoring: ODUkP overhead (non-intrusive)
• Trigger: ODU TSF/TSD (Trail Signal Fail/Degrade)
• MFAS bits 6-8: 000 (ODUk Path level)
• Advantage: End-to-end visibility
• Limitation: Cannot distinguish local vs remote faults
#5

1+1 Bidirectional • SNC/Ne

APS: 000 Server: Multiple HO ODUk

Similar to Scheme #4 but with bidirectional APS coordination (MFAS 000 = ODUk Path level). Both directions switch symmetrically when end-to-end path quality degrades.

#6

1+1 Unidirectional • SNC/Ns (Non-intrusive Sublayer)

No APS ODUkT Monitoring
Sublayer TCM Monitoring (Non-intrusive)

Non-intrusive Sublayer (SNC/Ns): Similar to SNC/Ne but monitors the ODUkT Tandem Connection Monitoring (TCM) sublayer instead of the end-to-end path.

🔍 TCM Monitoring:
• Reads TCM overhead without terminating
• Protected entity: ODUkT (Tandem)
• Trigger: ODU TSF/TSD at TCM level
• No APS required (unidirectional)
#7

1+1 Bidirectional • SNC/Ns

APS: 001-110 (TCM1-6) Bidirectional

Bidirectional version of Scheme #6 with APS coordination. MFAS bits 6-8 = 001-110 indicate which TCM level (TCM1 through TCM6) carries the APS channel.

#8

1+1 Unidirectional • SNC/S (Sublayer with TCM)

No APS TCM Terminated
TCM Sublayer Termination (Intrusive)

Sublayer Monitoring (SNC/S): The RECOMMENDED approach. Dedicates one of the six TCM levels (TCM1-TCM6) specifically to the protection subnetwork.

✓ Why SNC/S is Recommended:
• Fault Isolation: Only monitors the protected segment
• Ignores upstream faults: Outside protected zone
• Trigger: ODUkT SSF/SSD (TCM level)
• Protected Entity: ODUkT or ODUkP
• MFAS: Not applicable (no APS)
#9

1+1 Bidirectional • SNC/S

APS: 001-110 (TCM1-6) Most Used

Most commonly deployed protection scheme. Bidirectional version of Scheme #8 with APS coordination. TCM sublayer provides isolated fault monitoring, and APS (carried on the same TCM level, MFAS 001-110) ensures symmetric bidirectional switching.

#10

1:n Bidirectional • SNC/S

Shared Protection APS: 001-110

Combines 1:n shared protection (like Scheme #3) with TCM sublayer monitoring (SNC/S). N working entities monitored via TCM share one protection entity. APS Byte 2 resolves which signal gets protection.

#11

1+1 Unidirectional • CL-SNCG/I (Compound Link Group)

No APS Group Protection
HO ODUk Protects Multiple LO ODUs

Compound Link Group (CL-SNCG/I): Protects multiple Low-Order ODUs (LO ODU) by monitoring the Higher-Order ODUk (HO ODUk) server layer that carries them.

🔗 Group Protection Concept:
• Server Layer: One HO ODUk (e.g., ODU4)
• Protected Entity: All LO ODUs multiplexed inside
• Trigger: HO ODUkP SSF/SSD
• Efficiency: Single protection path for many clients
• Use Case: HO/LO muxing with group switching
#12

1+1 Bidirectional • CL-SNCG/I

APS: HO 000 Group + Bidir

Bidirectional version of Scheme #11. APS coordination at HO level (MFAS HO 000 = HO ODUk Path). All LO ODUs switch symmetrically when HO container fails.

#13

1:1 Bidirectional • CL-SNCG/I (with Extra Traffic)

APS: HO 000 Switched Bridge

1:1 architecture with compound link group. Switched bridge (not permanent) allows protection HO container to carry Extra Traffic during normal operation. When HO working fails, bridge switches and preempts Extra Traffic to restore the entire LO ODU group.
